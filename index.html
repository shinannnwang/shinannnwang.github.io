Shinan Wang

PhD Candidate · Management & Organizations & Sociology
Kellogg School of Management · Northwestern University

I study how technology reshapes work, careers, and opportunity. As new technologies change what employers value, who gets ahead? One idea runs through my work: what a worker's skills are worth is not fixed. Technology and social context keep changing it. For firms, that makes talent a question of competitive advantage: how they build, value, and keep their human capital.

In my job market paper, I show that major technological revolutions reshape career hierarchies by opening temporary mobility windows: when a new technology arrives, past accomplishments stop signaling future contribution, and junior workers move past senior peers. In a paper published in Administrative Science Quarterly, my coauthors and I show that remote work raises the skills and qualifications employers require.

My broader work turns from technology to the social fabric of work. Across more than 100 countries, I study how social ties, social capital, and kinship shape who firms hire, who collaborates, and who becomes an entrepreneur. These questions matter most for multinational firms, which manage talent across very different societies.

My work combines large-scale data and computational methods with historical archives, online experiments, and qualitative interviews.

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.
